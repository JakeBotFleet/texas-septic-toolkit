# Should you repair a failing septic system, or replace it?

> Price both paths before you pay for a second major repair. A repair can replace broken parts, but it cannot fix drainfield soil that has stopped absorbing water.

Reviewed **2026-11-03**.

Use this question list with a licensed installer, and with the permitting authority where a permit decision is involved:

1. What exactly failed? Ask which part is broken: a tank component, a line, a pump, or the drainfield itself. If the problem is new and nobody has diagnosed it yet, record the symptoms first; the [warning signs guide](septic-warning-signs-when-to-call.md) shows what to write down.
2. Is the drainfield still absorbing? If the soil in the drainfield no longer takes water, replacing parts upstream does not solve the problem. Ask what evidence supports the answer, such as ponding over the field, backups in the house, or inspection findings.
3. How old is the system, and what has already been repaired? Age alone does not decide the question. A pattern matters more: repeated backups after pumping, or a second major repair in a short span, points toward pricing a replacement. Your service records help here; the [maintenance guide](septic-maintenance-schedule-homeowners.md) lists what to keep.
4. Has anyone evaluated the whole system, or only the broken part? Get an evaluation before paying for a second major repair, so the decision rests on the condition of the tank, the lines, and the field together.
5. What would a replacement require here? A replacement system needs a permit and a site evaluation. Confirm the process with the permitting authority; the [Palo Pinto County permitting guide](tceq-ossf-permitting-palo-pinto-county.md) walks through it, and TCEQ's [permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html) explains the authority's role.
6. Are the two quotes scoped the same way? Compare repair and replacement on the same scope: the same parts, the same permit steps, the same ground restoration afterward. A low repair quote that leaves a failing drainfield untouched is a different offer from a full replacement.
7. What does waiting cost? Ask what continued failure risks while you decide: backups into the house, sewage at the surface, and a repair bill spent on a system that still gets replaced next year.

This page is a question list for a repair decision, not a diagnosis, a design, or a price estimate. Only a licensed professional who evaluates the system, and the permitting authority where a permit applies, can say which path a specific property needs.
