# What should you check before building an addition, pool, or shop near a septic system?

> Find the system on paper before you design around it. Setbacks and the drainfield's spare ground decide where new construction can go.

Reviewed **2026-11-03**.

Use this question list with your installer and the permitting authority before design work starts:

1. Where is the system, exactly? Ask for the permit record and the approved plan, which show where the tank and drainfield were actually built. Memory, a lid in the yard, and a seller's sketch are not enough to design against.
2. What setbacks apply to this lot? The distances from the tank and drainfield to structures, water wells, pools, and property lines are set by rule. The permitting authority confirms the distances for your lot; the [Palo Pinto County permitting guide](tceq-ossf-permitting-palo-pinto-county.md) explains how to reach that office, and TCEQ's [permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html) explains its role.
3. Does the plan leave room for a future drainfield? The drainfield needs its full area, plus ground held in reserve for a replacement field if the first one fails. Building on the reserve area can leave nowhere legal to put a new field later.
4. Does anything sit on top of the system? Do not build, pave, or drive over tanks or drainfields, and route driveways around them. Weight and pavement can damage components, and buried parts still need access for pumping and repair.
5. Does the project add water use? An added bathroom, an added bedroom, or plumbing in a new shop raises the daily flow the system was sized for. Ask whether the existing system covers the new total before the addition is designed around it.
6. What site work comes first? Clearing and grading change how water drains around the field. The [site preparation guide](land-clearing-site-prep-before-septic.md) covers the order of dirt work, and the [soil research guide](palo-pinto-county-soil-types-septic.md) explains why soil decides what a lot can support.
7. Who approves the plan before money is spent? Confirm with the permitting authority whether the project needs approval or a change to the existing permit, and get that answer before construction starts.

This page is a planning question list, not a site plan, a setback ruling, or a permit. Only the permitting authority can confirm the distances and approvals for a specific lot.
