# How do I prepare for an OSSF permit in Palo Pinto County?

> Identify the authority for the parcel, obtain its current application packet, and arrange the site evaluation before treating a tool result as part of a proposed design.

Sources reviewed **2026-10-18**. Educational preparation guide; parcel jurisdiction and application approval must come from the permitting authority.

## Start with the official county page

[Palo Pinto County Public Works](https://www.co.palo-pinto.tx.us/page/PublicWorksDepartment) publishes its OSSF application, inspection-fee table, and application requirements. The page calls for an application, planning material, site-evaluation results, and the appropriate fee before authorization to construct. It identifies the Public Works office at **100 SE 6th Ave, Suite 212, Mineral Wells**, with office number **940-659-1240**.

Use that page's current fee table and forms. A generic statewide fee estimate, courthouse mailing address, or copied phone number is a poor substitute. Ask the office which fee category and supporting materials apply to the proposed work.

County location alone is not a complete jurisdiction check. Near Possum Kingdom Lake, use the [BRA jurisdiction guide](possum-kingdom-lake-septic-considerations.md). For other boundary questions, start with TCEQ's [permitting-authority lookup linked from its permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html).

## Prepare a record that the reviewer can use

This is a preparation checklist, not an official application form:

1. Identify the parcel using the survey, legal description, address, and map. Flag municipal boundaries, lake proximity, and existing permit numbers.
2. Describe the proposed use: dwelling/building information, additional connections, changes to occupancy or use, and whether work concerns a new or existing system.
3. Ask the authority which planning documents, professional qualifications, forms, fees, and inspections it requires for that work.
4. Arrange the preconstruction evaluation. TCEQ identifies a licensed site evaluator or professional engineer for this task; an installer license by itself is not evidence of that separate qualification. [TCEQ permitting guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html)
5. Keep the evaluation, proposed system information, and the source of every numerical input together. See [tool inputs and sources](tool-inputs-and-sources.md).
6. Obtain the applicable construction authorization before installation. Ask which stages must remain visible for inspection and how to document approved changes.
7. Retain the approved plans, authorization, inspection/correction records, and final operating documents supplied by the authority.

## Questions that prevent unsupported assumptions

- Which entity has authority over this exact parcel?
- Is a new application, modification, transfer, or another process appropriate?
- Who may prepare the particular planning material and perform each task?
- Which local requirements supplement state rules?
- What is the confirmed inspection sequence, and what may be covered afterward?
- What final document allows the proposed system to operate?

A spreadsheet cannot answer these administrative questions. This guide promises no approval date, inspection outcome, or exemption. TCEQ says local requirements may be more stringent and tells owners to confirm any claimed exemption with their authority. [TCEQ permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html)
