If you own land in Palo Pinto County, Texas, and you need a septic system for a new home, an accessory building, or a failed-system replacement, you are working under the TCEQ On-Site Sewage Facility (OSSF) program. The state rules live in 30 TAC Chapter 285, and the county-level permits are handled by a Designated Representative (DR). In Palo Pinto County, that office is inside the Palo Pinto County Public Works Department. This guide walks you through the actual steps, the fees, and the mistakes that delay permits on rural North Texas jobs.

> Quick answer: Submit a TCEQ OSSF application with a site plan and perc test to the Palo Pinto County Public Works Department, pay the county permit fee, and wait for DR review before any installation. Out West Septic (OS0041298) handles the application paperwork as part of every quote.

## What the application actually requires

The TCEQ OSSF application for Palo Pinto County expects:

1. A site plan drawn to scale showing property lines, structures, the proposed tank location, the proposed drainfield location, water well, surface water, driveways, and easements.
2. A soil or perc test report signed by a TCEQ-licensed Installer I, II, or III, or by the DR.
3. Design calculations. Daily design flow (150 gpd × bedrooms), minimum tank size, minimum absorption area, lateral count, lateral length.
4. A fee. Current Palo Pinto County pass-through is in the $200-$400 range. Confirm with the office.

For new construction, you also need a sewage permit application on the TCEQ form (current revision). For system modifications or repairs, the same form applies with a notation that the work is a modification, not a new install.

## The 10-step permit process

The actual process, in order:

1. Site evaluation. Out West Septic walks the lot with you. We confirm setbacks per Chapter 285: 100 ft from a private well, 150 ft from a public water supply, 50 ft from a property line, 10 ft from a foundation, 50 ft from surface water, 10 ft from a subsurface drain, 5 ft from utility lines, 15 ft from a swimming pool.
2. Soil or perc test. We dig one or more test holes, presoak, and run timed readings to get a perc rate (min/in). Conventional gravity systems need a perc rate between roughly 1 and 60 min/in.
3. Site plan. Drawn to scale, usually 1" = 20' or 1" = 30', with all setback dimensions called out.
4. Application. Submit to the Palo Pinto County Public Works Department along with the perc test, design calculations, and fee.
5. Pay permit fee. County pass-through. Confirm the current amount at the office.
6. DR review. The DR may request changes. Common requests: add a corner pin, expand setbacks, run a second perc hole, or revise the site plan to show easements we missed.
7. Install. Out West Septic installs the conventional gravity system per the approved design. The DR must inspect before backfill is complete.
8. Notify DR. Call the office when the install is ready for inspection. Lead time in Palo Pinto County is typically 1-3 business days.
9. DR inspection. The DR walks the install, signs off, and the completion paperwork is filed.
10. Completion paperwork. The DR files a completion report with the TCEQ. Keep your copy for resale, refinancing, or insurance.

## Common reasons permits get denied or delayed

In our experience in Palo Pinto County, the top reasons are:

- Incomplete setbacks. Easements are easy to miss. If there's a utility easement, the drainfield has to be outside it.
- Perc rate too slow. Thurber clay and similar soils can hit 45-60+ min/in. We may have to expand the drainfield significantly or recommend aerobic referral. Out West Septic installs conventional only and refers aerobic work.
- Perc rate too fast. Less than 1 min/in usually means the soil won't provide adequate treatment. The DR may require a sand mound or a specialized design.
- Tank size wrong. Three bedrooms gets you 1,000 gallons. Four gets you 1,250. Six gets you 1,500. Seven-plus adds 250 gal per bedroom beyond six.
- Insufficient lot size. TCEQ Chapter 285 has a minimum lot size for new OSSF systems in many counties. We confirm this at the site visit.
- Surface water within 50 ft. Stock tanks, ponds, creeks, and the BRA lakes count. Setback must be respected.
- No TCEQ-licensed installer on the application. Out West Septic (OS0041298) signs every Palo Pinto County application we file.
