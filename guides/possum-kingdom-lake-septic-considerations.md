Outside that boundary, the county's Designated Representative handles permits as usual.

## The BRA jurisdiction boundary at Possum Kingdom Lake

Possum Kingdom Lake is a Brazos River Authority reservoir. BRA holds OSSF permitting authority inside a defined boundary around the lake. The boundary is:

- **2,000 ft from the lake** at normal pool elevation, **OR**
- **1,000 ft above the 1,000-ft contour line**, whichever boundary captures more property.

In practice, the 1,000-ft contour often captures the uphill side and the 2,000-ft buffer captures the lakeside, so the BRA jurisdiction covers a continuous band around most of the lake.

The BRA OSSF rules are stricter than the county rules in a few important ways:

- **Enhanced setbacks** for the lake and for tributaries.
- **Aerobic treatment units (ATUs)** are commonly required, not optional.
- **Maintenance contracts** with a TCEQ-licensed maintenance provider are required for ATUs.
- **Annual inspections** of the ATU are filed with BRA.

If you're inside the BRA boundary and your soil is borderline, expect an ATU. If you're outside the boundary on a typical conventional lot, the county rules apply and a conventional system is usually fine.

## How to find out if your lot is in BRA jurisdiction

Three ways:

1. **Look at the BRA OSSF map.** Brazos River Authority maintains an OSSF jurisdiction map on their website. The boundary is publicly viewable.
2. **Call the BRA OSSF office.** They will tell you over the phone whether a specific address is in their jurisdiction.
3. **Ask Out West Septic.** When we do a site visit, we confirm the jurisdiction as part of the quote.

If you're early in the process and just shopping for land, ask the seller's agent or the title company. They usually know whether the lot is in BRA jurisdiction because it affects resale value.

## The conventional case near PK Lake

If your PK Lake lot is **outside** the BRA boundary — usually uphill and more than 2,000 ft from the lake — a conventional gravity system is the standard install. Same TCEQ Chapter 285 rules, same county DR, same Out West Septic install process.

The soil conditions near PK Lake are similar to the rest of Palo Pinto County:

- **Sandy loam** on the upland positions — easy conventional case.

## The application process inside BRA jurisdiction

The BRA OSSF process is similar to the county process but with BRA-specific forms and BRA-specific fees. The general flow is:

1. **Site evaluation** by a TCEQ-licensed Installer (Out West Septic handles this for conventional components).
2. **Soil/perc test** — same TCEQ-accepted methodology.
3. **Site plan** drawn to scale.
4. **Application** to BRA OSSF office (instead of the county).
5. **BRA review and approval.**
6. **Install** by a TCEQ-licensed Installer.
7. **BRA inspection** before backfill.
8. **Completion paperwork** filed with BRA.
9. **ATU maintenance contract** filed with BRA (if ATU).

If you're building a new home on a PK Lake lot inside the BRA boundary, expect the timeline to be slightly longer than a county install because of the extra review step.
