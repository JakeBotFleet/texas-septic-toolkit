On every new conventional septic install in rural North Texas, the dirt work comes first, and it's not optional. This guide explains why land clearing, building pad prep, and drainfield site prep have to happen before the tank goes in, what each step actually does, and how the sequencing affects the cost.

> Quick answer: Land clearing and site prep have to happen before tank and drainfield installation because the excavator needs clear, accessible ground to dig. Trying to install septic into an uncleared lot means hand-digging, slower work, and a higher risk of hitting rock or root systems. Out West Septic handles dirt work and septic install together. ## The sequence on a typical rural North Texas new build

For a new home in Palo Pinto County, Hood County, Erath County, or anywhere in our service area, the order is:

1. Land clearing. Remove trees, brush, and stumps from the build area. Push the topsoil aside for later.
2. Building pad prep. Cut and fill to create a level pad for the home. Compact.
3. Driveway. Cut in the driveway so equipment can access the site.
4. Septic site prep. Clear the drainfield area and tank area. Stage the soil.
5. Septic install. Out West Septic digs the tank hole and the lateral trenches, sets the tank, lays the laterals, calls for DR inspection.
6. Backfill and restoration. Topsoil, seed, straw over the disturbed area.

If the septic install is on a lot with an existing home, the sequence is simpler, usually just steps 4-6, with whatever clearing is needed to access the drainfield area.

## What land clearing actually involves

On a typical rural Texas lot:

- Push down trees and brush in the build area. Stumps are removed or ground depending on the site.
- Stack or burn brush per local rules (burn bans are common in summer. Check with the county).
- Strip topsoil from the build area and stockpile it for later restoration. Topsoil is the most valuable part of the soil profile and you want it back over the disturbed area when the work is done.
- Cut and fill to level the building pad. On a flat lot this is minimal. On a rolling lot it can be significant.

Land clearing in Palo Pinto County typically runs $1,500-$3,500 per acre depending on tree density, slope, and access. See our [North Texas cost guide](./septic-costs-north-texas-2026.md) for typical ranges.

## What building pad prep involves

The pad is the level surface the home sits on. For a typical rural Texas home:

- Cut high spots and fill low spots to create a level pad.
- Compact the fill to prevent settling.
- Slope the pad slightly so water drains away from the future home footprint.

Out West Septic handles building pad prep as part of the same dirt-work package as the septic install. One excavator, one schedule, one mobilization fee.

## What septic site prep involves

After the building pad is in but before the tank goes in:

- Stake out the tank location and the drainfield location per the approved site plan.
- Clear the access path so the excavator can reach both areas.
- Strip and stockpile topsoil from the drainfield area for restoration later.
- Pre-mark any easements so the drainfield doesn't end up in a utility easement.

If the soil in the drainfield area is rocky or has tight clay, we may need to do extra excavation work to deal with it. That's when a soil series identification (see our [Palo Pinto County soil guide](./palo-pinto-county-soil-types-septic.md)) really pays off.

## Why sequencing matters

If you try to install the septic system before the dirt work is done, you run into:

- Access problems. The excavator can't get to the drainfield area.
- Rock and stump encounters that would have been removed during clearing.
- No place to stage soil. The tank hole and trench spoil have to go somewhere.
- Risk of damaging the new system later when the building pad work happens.

If you try to install the septic system after the home is built, you risk:

- Driving heavy equipment across the yard and damaging the landscape.
- Working around the home with a big excavator.
- Higher labor cost because everything is tighter and slower.

Doing dirt work and septic install together, in the right sequence, is the cheapest path.

## Rock encounters change the math

If we hit rock during excavation (common in Palo Pinto County on Bonti-Exray and similar series), the rock adder applies. Typical rock adder in our quotes is $50-$150 per cubic yard of rock removed. We itemize this on every quote so you know the rock adder is real cost, not padding.

Some lots are rocky throughout. Some have rock at one depth and then open up below. The site visit tells us roughly what to expect, but the actual encounter is what drives the final number.

## What a site visit looks at

When we walk your lot before quoting:

- Trees and brush in the build area. What needs to come out.
- Slope and drainage. Where water wants to go, where the pad needs to be.
- Soil series. Cisco sandy loam is easy, Thurber clay is hard, Bonti-Exray is shallow limestone.
- Access. Can we get a 20-ton excavator to the build area, or do we need to use smaller equipment?
- Easements. Utility, drainage, access. These constrain where the drainfield can go.
- Existing well location. Setbacks from the well matter for the drainfield.

The site visit is where the whole project gets scoped. We leave with a real number, not a guess.
