Conventional septic systems don't fail all at once. They give you warning signs — sometimes weeks or years before a complete failure. Knowing what to watch for in your Palo Pinto County, Hood County, or Erath County home can save you thousands of dollars by catching problems early. This is what Out West Septic sees on actual repair calls across rural North Texas.

> **Quick answer:** Five signs that mean "call now": (1) slow drains throughout the house, (2) gurgling or bubbling in toilets and tubs, (3) wet spots or sewage odor over the drainfield, (4) backups into the lowest drains, (5) unusually green grass over the drainfield. All five indicate the tank is overdue for pumping or the drainfield is failing.

## 1. Slow drains throughout the house

If one sink drains slowly, it's probably a clogged P-trap — not a septic problem. If **every** drain in the house is slow at the same time, that's the septic system.

What it usually means:

- The tank is overdue for pumping and solids are restricting flow out of the tank.
- The drainfield is saturated and can't accept the flow.
- There's a clog in the line between the house and the tank.

What to do:

- Try to remember when you last had the tank pumped. If it's been more than 3–5 years, pumping is the first step.
- If pumping doesn't fix it, the next step is a drainfield inspection. Out West Septic runs those for **$250–$500**.
- Avoid using the system heavily until the issue is resolved. Spread out laundry, limit shower length, and skip the garbage disposal.

## 2. Gurgling or bubbling in toilets and tubs

When you run water somewhere in the house and hear gurgling in a toilet or see bubbles in a tub drain, the system is trying to breathe through fixtures because the path downstream is restricted.

What it usually means:

- The tank is full or the drainfield is saturated.
- There's a partial clog in the line between the tank and the drainfield.
- The vent stack on the roof is blocked (less common, but possible — leaves, a bird nest).

What to do:

- Check the roof vent for blockages if you can safely access it.
- If pumping is overdue, get the tank pumped.
- If gurgling persists, schedule an inspection.

## 3. Wet spots or sewage odor over the drainfield

This is the most urgent sign. If you can see wet soil, smell sewage, or both over the drainfield, the system is failing.

What it usually means:

- The drainfield is saturated and effluent is surfacing.
- There's a clog or break in the lateral lines.
- The tank is overflowing because the drainfield won't take the flow.

What to do:

- **Reduce water use immediately.** Spread laundry out, take short showers, fix any running toilets.
- **Don't drive or park over the drainfield.**
- **Call Out West Septic at 940.452.0598** for an inspection. Surfacing effluent is a health hazard and a regulatory issue.
- **Don't wait.** A surfacing drainfield can contaminate surface water or a well if left untreated.

The fix ranges from a localized lateral repair (**$3,000–$8,000**) to a full drainfield replacement (**$4,000–$10,000**), depending on what we find.

## 4. Backups into the lowest drains

If sewage backs up into a shower, a basement floor drain, or a low-elevation toilet when you run water elsewhere in the house, the system is overwhelmed.

What it usually means:

- The tank is full and the drainfield is not accepting flow.
- There's a clog between the house and the tank.
- A septic filter is clogged (if the system has one).

What to do:

- Stop running water into the system until it's resolved.
- If you know where the tank is and can safely access the inspection port, you can check the water level in the tank yourself. If the water level is at or near the top of the tank, the system is overwhelmed.
- Call Out West Septic at 940.452.0598. We can usually get to a Palo Pinto County backup call within 24 hours.

The fix is usually pumping plus a localized repair (**$2,500–$5,000** depending on what we find).

## 5. Unusually green grass over the drainfield

If the grass over your drainfield is dramatically greener than the rest of the yard, the drainfield is surfacing effluent. The grass is using the nutrients in the effluent as fertilizer.

What it usually means:

- The drainfield is failing.
- The tank is overflowing.
- A lateral line is broken or disconnected.

What to do:

- Treat this as an urgent sign — same as wet spots or odor over the drainfield.
- Call Out West Septic at 940.452.0598.
- Reduce water use in the house.
