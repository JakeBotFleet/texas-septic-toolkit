# What should I record when a septic problem appears?

> Record what you observed and arrange appropriate professional help. A symptom list or calculator cannot identify the cause or certify that a system is safe.

Reviewed **2026-10-18**.

Useful observations include the time and location of an alarm, sewage backup, persistent odor, unusually wet ground, or a change in drainage. Keep people away from exposed sewage and unsecured access openings; do not enter a tank or excavation to investigate.

Record recent household-use changes, weather, work near the system, and the last service visit. Give the professional the permit, approved plan, equipment details, and service records if available. Ask for the observed condition, proposed action, and whether authority approval is needed before repairs.

TCEQ's [homeowner resources](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfhomeowners.html) link to maintenance, licensed-professional searches, permitting authorities, and complaint guidance. Follow the [manufacturer and applicable maintenance requirements](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfmaintenance.html) for an alarm or malfunction.

This page offers a reporting checklist, not emergency diagnosis, a repair prescription, a response-time promise, or an Out West Septic service commitment.
