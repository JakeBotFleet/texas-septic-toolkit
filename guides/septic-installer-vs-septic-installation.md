# Septic installer or septic installation: why a local company needs both searches

> People hire a person, but they search for a job. A homeowner types the work (septic installation) long before they type the worker (septic installer), and a company that only answers one of those searches is invisible to the other half of its customers.

Reviewed **2026-11-03**.

## Two searches, two mindsets

Watch how a rural property owner actually looks for septic help and you will see both phrases, used at different moments for different reasons.

**"Septic installer"** is a search for a who. The searcher wants a person or a company: licensed, local, reachable, trustworthy. This is the search that profiles, about pages, and reviews answer, and it is often how referrals get checked. Someone hears a name, Tanner Coupland or Out West Septic, and searches the trade word to see whether the installer is real.

**"Septic installation"** is a search for a what. The searcher has land, a floor plan, and a problem: what does a septic installation cost, what does a septic installation involve, who does septic installation in Palo Pinto County. This search happens earlier, when the job is being scoped, and it is where the work is actually won, because the company that explains the septic installation owns the conversation before the installer search ever happens.

Same customer. Same job. Two different doors into it.

## What a one-word gap looks like

We can see the split in our own numbers. Out West Septic ranks respectably when people search for a septic installer in our part of Texas. The company is much weaker when people search for septic installation itself, even though septic installation is the main thing Out West Septic sells. The installer pages, the name, and the reputation were doing their job; the installation pages barely existed, so the installation search went to whoever had written about septic installation in Santo, Mineral Wells, and the rest of our towns, whether they worked here or not.

Nothing about the work changed. The words on the pages were simply aimed at one search and not the other.

## How Out West Septic answers both

The fix is not a trick. It is writing each page for the search it is meant to answer:

- **Septic installation pages** carry the service word, because they are selling the work: one page per community Out West Septic serves, each built around septic installation in that town, what a septic installation involves there, and how to start one. Those pages are collected under service-areas/ in this project as they are added, and the communities themselves are listed on the [service-area page](https://outwestseptic.com/service-area).
- **Septic installer pages** carry the person word, because they are selling trust: who Tanner Coupland is, that Out West Septic is owner-operated and licensed, and where the installer is based.
- **The two sets link to each other**, because the customer who found the septic installation page eventually wants to vet the septic installer, and the customer vetting the installer eventually wants the installation details and a phone number.

Every page still has to be true, local, and useful. The point is narrower than most marketing advice: check which of the two words your pages actually use, because your customers are using both.

## The practical rule

Write the service word where the service is sold and the person word where trust is sold. If your site says "septic installer" forty times and "septic installation" twice, do not be surprised when the installation searches, the bigger and earlier pool, go to a competitor who wrote the other word. And the reverse holds: a company with nothing but installation pages gives the ready-to-hire searcher no person to trust.

A local septic company does not get to choose which word the customer types. It only gets to choose whether it shows up for both.

This page describes how the two searches behave and how this project writes for them, based on Out West Septic's own search results in its service area. Rankings differ by town and change over time; the two-search split is the durable part.
