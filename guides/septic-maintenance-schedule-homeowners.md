# How often should you pump a septic tank in Texas?

Out West Septic, outwestseptic.com, TCEQ OSSF Installer I, License OS0041298, Lone Camp, TX, 940.452.0598

The honest answer for how often to pump a septic tank in Texas is "it depends." On tank size, household size, whether you have a garbage disposal, and whether you have a water softener. This guide gives you a defensible interval and explains what to do (and not do) to keep your system healthy.

> Quick answer: Most conventional septic tanks in North Texas need pumping every 3 to 5 years. Smaller tanks with larger households pump on the short end. Larger tanks with smaller households can go longer. Out West Septic does not run a pump truck. We refer pumping to local pumpers and provide the schedule.

## The simple rule of thumb

A conventional septic tank in good working order should be pumped when the sludge layer reaches about 1/3 of the tank capacity or when the scum layer is within 6 inches of the outlet. Without measuring, you can't know for sure, so we recommend pumping on a schedule based on what we see in Palo Pinto County:

| Tank Size | Household | Pump Interval |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1,000 gal | 2 people | about 5 years |
| 1,000 gal | 4 people | 2-3 years |
| 1,000 gal | 6 people | 1.5-2 years |
| 1,250 gal | 2 people | about 6 years |
| 1,250 gal | 4 people | about 3 years |
| 1,250 gal | 6 people | about 2 years |
| 1,500 gal | 2 people | about 7 years |
| 1,500 gal | 4 people | 3.5 years |
| 1,500 gal | 6 people | 2.5 years |

Numbers above assume no garbage disposal. Add a disposal, knock 30-40% off the interval. Add a water softener, knock another 10-15% off.

## What we recommend

For a typical 1,000-1,250 gallon tank with a 3-4 person household in Palo Pinto County:

- Pump every 3 years if you have a garbage disposal.
- Pump every 4 years if you don't have a disposal but you do have a water softener.
- Pump every 5 years if you have neither.

These are conservative. Going significantly longer risks solids leaving the tank and clogging the drainfield. A much more expensive problem than a $300 pump.

## What homeowners do that kills tanks

The list of things not to do is more important than the things to do:

- Don't flush wipes. Even "flushable" wipes don't break down in a septic tank. They clog the tank, the filter, and the drainfield.
- Don't pour grease down the drain. It solidifies in the tank and reduces effective capacity.
- Don't use a garbage disposal as a trash can. Every load of food waste becomes solids in the tank. Compost or bag it instead.
- Don't do all your laundry in one day. Spread it out. A huge slug of water can stir up the tank and push solids into the drainfield.
- Don't park or drive over the tank or drainfield. Compacted soil reduces drainage. Out West Septic marks tank and drainfield locations on every install.
- Don't plant trees near the drainfield. Roots will find the moist soil and grow into the laterals.
- Don't direct roof gutters or surface drains over the drainfield. Saturated soil kills the drainfield.
- Don't use harsh chemical drain cleaners. They can disrupt the bacterial action in the tank.

## What homeowners do that helps

- Spread laundry over the week. Multiple small loads beat one giant load.
- Fix leaks promptly. A running toilet can double the household's water load on the system.
- Use septic-safe toilet paper. It breaks down faster.
- Keep a record of pump dates. A simple spreadsheet or notebook beats memory.
- Get an inspection every 2-3 years. Out West Septic does inspections for $250-$500. A small price to catch problems before they become drainfield replacements.
- Install risers if your tank doesn't have them. Easier pump access, easier inspection access. Out West Septic adds risers on most new installs.

## How to know if your tank is overdue

Signs your tank is overdue for pumping:

- Slow drains throughout the house.
- Gurgling sounds in the plumbing when fixtures drain.
- Sewer odor in the house or yard.
- Wet spots over the drainfield.
- Backups in the lowest drains in the house.

If you see any of these, get the tank pumped first. It may be all you need. If pumping doesn't fix it, the next step is a drainfield inspection. Out West Septic can run that for you.

## The TCEQ rule of thumb

The TCEQ and Texas A&M AgriLife both recommend pumping at least every 3 to 5 years for a conventional system serving a typical household. That's the public-facing guidance and it's a safe default.
