# How should I plan septic maintenance?

> Use the system's records, manufacturer instructions, applicable permit conditions, and professional observations. A calendar estimate is not a diagnosis.

Reviewed **2026-10-18**.

TCEQ's [maintenance guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfmaintenance.html) recommends pumping conventional tanks every three to five years. For aerobic units it points to sludge depth and manufacturer recommendations. Its separate [owner-advice table](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfadvice.html) presents intervals between **pumpings or inspections** for tank/household combinations; those numbers should not be treated as a guarantee that waiting is safe.

Create a record with:

- System type, permit, approved plan, equipment information, and manufacturer instructions.
- Inspection, pumping, repair, alarm, and maintenance dates.
- Professional findings, recommended next actions, and the basis for the next visit.
- Any required maintenance contract and reporting obligations.

For aerobic and other covered systems, local requirements can affect who may maintain the system. Confirm the applicable requirements with the authority rather than assuming a universal annual visit or lifetime contract rule. [TCEQ maintenance guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfmaintenance.html)

If a tool produces a reminder, label it as a planning reminder and retain its source. Symptoms or alarms need attention independently of that date; see [warning signs](septic-warning-signs-when-to-call.md). The affiliated [Out West Septic](https://outwestseptic.com) project does not certify maintenance intervals through this repository.
