# Do I need a conventional or aerobic septic system in Texas?

Texas homeowners shopping for a new septic system usually end up choosing between two main types: conventional gravity and aerobic treatment unit (ATU). The choice isn't really about preference. It's driven by lot size, soil conditions, and how close the property sits to a lake, creek, or water well. This guide walks through the actual decision so you can show up to a quote conversation with Out West Septic knowing what's likely.

> Quick answer: Conventional gravity is the right choice for the vast majority of rural North Texas lots when you have enough land and acceptable soil. Aerobic systems are required when the soil won't drain, the lot is too small for a conventional drainfield, or you're within a regulated lake setback.

## What "conventional" actually means in Texas

A conventional gravity system in Texas is what most rural homeowners picture:

- A septic tank (concrete, fiberglass, or polyethylene) that holds solids and lets clarified effluent flow out.
- A drainfield (also called a leach field or absorption field) made of perforated pipe in gravel- or chamber-filled trenches. The soil under the trenches treats the effluent before it reaches groundwater.

Conventional systems are passive. No electricity, no moving parts, no annual maintenance contract. They are governed by 30 TAC Chapter 285 minimums, which set things like tank size, drainfield absorption area, and setback distances.

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) maintains the rule book at the [On-Site Sewage Facility program page](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf).

## What an aerobic system adds

An aerobic treatment unit (ATU) is a more complex tank that injects air into the wastewater, breaking down solids faster and producing a higher-quality effluent. ATUs are required when:

- The soil perc rate is too slow for conventional (above 60 min/in in most of North Texas).
- The lot is too small for a conventional drainfield. TCEQ has minimum lot sizes that vary by county.
- You're inside a regulated setback near a lake, reservoir, or water supply, where higher effluent quality is required.

Aerobic systems in Texas:

- Run on electricity (an air pump or aerator).
- Require a maintenance contract with a TCEQ-licensed maintenance provider.
- Have annual or biannual inspections filed with the county.
- Cost more up front and more over time than conventional.

In Possum Kingdom Lake, for example, the Brazos River Authority is the OSSF authority within 2,000 ft of the lake or the 1,000-ft contour line, and ATUs are commonly required inside that boundary.

## How to decide for a North Texas lot

The decision tree is roughly:

1. Is the lot 1 acre or more, with at least 100 ft of usable frontage outside setbacks?
   - Yes. Conventional is likely feasible.
   - No. Check minimum lot size for your county. ATU may be required.

2. What does the soil do?
   - Sandy loam, loam, well-drained clay loam. Conventional works.
   - Tight clay (Thurber, Windthorst) with perc rates above 60 min/in. Conventional may not work. Expect ATU referral or significantly expanded drainfield.

3. Are there setback concerns?
   - Inside 2,000 ft of Possum Kingdom Lake or 1,000-ft contour. BRA jurisdiction. Expect ATU.
   - 100 ft or more from a private well, 150 ft from public water supply, 50 ft from property line, 10 ft from foundation, 50 ft from surface water. Conventional setback check passes.

4. Do you have electricity at the site?
   - Yes. Both options remain open.
   - No. Conventional is the practical choice until power is run.

5. How much do you want to spend over 10 years?
   - Conventional: lower install cost, zero electricity, no annual contract.
   - Aerobic: higher install cost (often $3,000-$7,000 more), electricity bill (about $10-$30/month), annual contract ($300-$500/year).

A conventional system in Palo Pinto County typically runs $5,000-$12,000 total installed, depending on tank size, drainfield length, soil conditions, and rock encountered. See our [North Texas cost guide](./septic-costs-north-texas-2026.md) for the full breakdown.

Aerobic systems in North Texas typically run $8,000-$18,000 total installed, plus the recurring maintenance contract and electricity. The cost gap is significant. Conventional wins on price in the right soil.

## Maintenance differences

A conventional septic system:

- Pump the tank every 2-6 years depending on household size.
- No electricity, no moving parts.
- No annual contract required by TCEQ.

An aerobic system:

- Annual TCEQ-required inspection by a licensed maintenance provider.
- Sludge removal every 2-4 years.
- Air pump replacement every 5-10 years.
- Continuous electricity for the aerator.
- Contract cost is recurring. Budget $300-$500/year for the life of the system.

## When conventional is clearly the right choice

- You have at least 1 acre of usable land.
- Soil perc rate is between 1 and 60 min/in.
- You're outside regulated lake or water setbacks.
- You want low long-term cost and no recurring contract.
- You're in Palo Pinto, Hood, Erath, Stephens, Young, Jack, or Eastland County and outside BRA jurisdiction near Possum Kingdom Lake.

## When aerobic referral is the right answer

- Lot is too small for a conventional drainfield per county minimums.
- Soil perc rate is above 60 min/in (very tight clay).
- Property is inside a regulated setback near Possum Kingdom Lake or another protected water body.
- TCEQ or the local Designated Representative specifies ATU on the permit.
